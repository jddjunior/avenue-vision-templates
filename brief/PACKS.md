# Three template packs from competitor studies

Each pack is 4 page templates for one fictional brand. The pack studies a real franchise's site for STRUCTURE and CONVERSION MECHANICS only. Never copy their brand: no brand names, slogans, trademarks, mascots, logos, or their color schemes. Banned phrases include "Clean It Like You Mean It", "Flip the Script", "the window franchise Promise", "America's On-Time Electrician", "StraightForward Pricing", "Punctual Plumber(s)", "Malarky", "Sparks Fly", "U-Win", "Ben the plumbing franchise". Write original copy.

General rules: follow `TEMPLATE_BRIEF.md` (same folder) exactly for file format and copy rules, EXCEPT page length: these are pack pages, so keep each page to 8–10 sections and about 3,000–4,500 px tall at 1280. Set `$preview` height accordingly (round up).

All pages of one pack must look like one site: same header, footer, fonts, colors, button styles, logo. Use exactly the pack's "Shared chrome" spec below.

## PACK A · "Crew Brand" (studied: [study site], a window and exterior cleaning franchise)

What we learned from the study:
- Personality-led brand built around a uniformed crew and their vans; retro-americana display type with offset shadow; photo cards with rounded corners and a thin dark border; an Instagram-style photo wall; crew culture ("character over experience") in hiring.
- Conversion: one big promise, "free quote within 1 business day"; a ZIP lookup that routes to the local franchise; a long multi-service quote form (service checkboxes, frequency one-time to monthly, "how did you hear about us" including "saw our van", SMS consent).
- Trust: four promise tiles (fast quotes, insurance coverage amounts stated, a fixed arrival window instead of a vague one, respect for the property: shoe covers, wiping sills, careful ladders); a three-point crew promise; three named reviews; press/podcast page; merch shop; parent-company brand family.
- SEO gaps to FIX in our pack: no individual service pages (every service just jumps to the quote form); location H1s are the brand name instead of "service + city"; location pages have no address, hours or license; service-area towns are missing on most locations; no FAQ; no local service pages.

Fictional brand: **Clearday Window & Wash** (franchise). Sample location: **Clearday St. Petersburg**, phone (727) 555-0161, owners [OWNER NAMES]. Brand line for quotes: "Free quote within 1 business day".

## PACK B · "On-Time Electric" (studied: [study site], an electrical franchise)

What we learned:
- National home with a ZIP finder that routes to the local franchise ("we don't service your ZIP yet" fallback); a deep service taxonomy in a mega-menu: Repairs & Emergency 24/7, Power & Panels, Switches & Outlets, Lighting & Fans, Smart Home, EV & Generators, Commercial; 30+ service pages at `/services/{category}/{service}/`.
- Promises: an on-time guarantee (late = repair free), a 100% satisfaction guarantee, upfront pricing before work, 24/7 emergency service, background-checked and drug-tested techs; a massive aggregate review count; humorous brand video; electrical safety resource hub; expert tips blog; membership club (annual inspection, priority scheduling, 10% off repairs, waived trip charge) with price hidden.
- Location pages `/tampa/`: H1 "Tampa Electrician", NAP with street address, 24/7 hours and the state license number, 12+ areas served at `/areas-we-service/{town-fl}/`, local offers, local review count, "Questions to ask when hiring an electrician", 8 local FAQs.
- SEO/UX gaps to FIX: national service pages have no local context, no FAQ, no price ranges, no comparison tables, no timeline/permit info; the location mixes local and national service URLs; membership price hidden.
